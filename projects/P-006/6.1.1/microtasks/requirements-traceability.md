# Requirements traceability

## MT14 — Requirements traceability, review and approval

Traceability lets a reviewer start from an objective or from a receipt and find the claims that it can support. For example, approval of an accurate employee preview depends on the current facts and policy, the people authorized to confirm them, and observations of the server decision and preview. The baseline provides the individual requirements, invariants, diagram, tests and closure. This supplement joins those records and identifies the decisions that still need owners. [Requirements and invariants](../security-requirements.md) · [Boundary model](../trust-boundaries.md) · [Design closure](../closure-report.md)

The two forward tables share the requirement ID. Read them together from objective and asset through owner and acceptance. All owners are proposed roles unless an isolated run documents the assignment. All human requirement approvals in this supplement are `PENDING`. Proposed priority **High** indicates a backend admission, containment or evidence condition. **Standard** indicates a model-quality target that still needs evaluation even when the backend contains a mistake. The priority does not alter the acceptance criteria. [Owner and approval decisions](review-and-acceptance.md#decisions-still-open)

T cases have distinct identifiers from the micro-tasks. A link from a risk or invariant to a requirement is a design relationship, with verification status determined by the corresponding evidence.

**How to verify and use it.** The learner should follow REQ-07 from its business objective to Phase B's decision and state receipts, then start from a Phase C event receipt and work back to REQ-08, INV-06 and the applicable privacy objective. Check where a claim stops and whose decision would allow it to go further. The trace meets documentary acceptance when each requirement resolves to an objective, known role or ownership gap, observation or test gap, acceptance and evidence, and each original invariant, boundary and test resolves back to a requirement. A consultant uses this chain to review evidence, hand off residual risks and seek scoped acceptance. The named roles and formal reviews still require confirmation.
